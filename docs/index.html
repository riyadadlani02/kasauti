Why kasauti?

Benchmarks ask short, independent questions. Agents run long trajectories where errors compound instead of averaging out.

The crossing point

At 4-bit, perplexity improved 4.6% and knowledge recall moved 4% — while format stability fell 24% and long-horizon adherence 19%.

Routing is discrete

A gate picks which experts handle each token. Nudge its scores and a token near the boundary flips entirely — a different computational path, not a noisier one.

27 configs, measured

Bit-width sweep, component ablations, group sizes, two methods. Every number on this page is a real measurement.

The flips are not random

They land almost entirely on tokens where the router was already torn. At 8-bit: one in ten of the most-undecided tokens flips, and none of the most-decided ones do.

No LLM judge

Every probe has mechanical ground truth. A quantized judge is circular and an API judge adds variance you cannot bound.

Minutes, not hours

One forward pass per calibration text against the base model. No agentic eval bill.

You're the engineer. Ship it?

Real measured configurations. You see the readings a practitioner actually consults — then what the agentic probes found.

Routing, rerouted

Every token is sent to 8 of 32 experts. Quantize the weights and the gate changes its mind — on 49% of tokens at 4-bit.

What each instrument sees

Both checks a practitioner consults stay flat — one improves — while the capabilities an agent depends on fall away beneath them.

Five things it now amends about itself

Every one of these was mine at some point, and every one changed the agent's answer when I fixed it. That is why they are no longer mine.

Its judge

Which probes it may grade on, and how. From its own record it re-derives all three fixes I had made by hand — and one I had got wrong.

Its test suite

When an audit cannot resolve a verdict it grows the suite that would. 78 → 188 items in this run, driven by the arithmetic of standard error.

Its decision threshold

Told to decide at 0.02 with an instrument that reads ±0.066, it moved the line to what it can defend — and logged why.

Its search policy

The bit-width ladder, and whether a failed config closes the route beyond it. Both amended from defects its own trace proved.

Its cheap signal

The cut points it searches on, refit against its own audit record — or, when the labels are unresolvable, a refusal to fit at all.

What stays mine

The criteria by which it judges its judge: what counts as no headroom, as confounded, as masking. It searches thoroughly inside my definition of a good auditor. It cannot decide that definition is wrong — and the section at the end of this page shows what happens when you let it try.

It searches, and it audits itself

The agent walks each component's bit-width down, scoring candidates on the cheap signal — then stops trusting that signal and pays for a real probe run.

16 steps 28 model evaluations 5 audits it could not resolve 0 evaluations on a second run
Result: gate 11 · attention 8 · expert 5 — 5.18 mean bits, 68% smaller than BF16, auditing at 0.961 ± 0.068 against a 0.95 floor. Uniform 4-bit, the configuration a practitioner reaches for, scores 0.454 on the same audit.

Five of the sixteen steps came back unresolved rather than failed — closer to the line than the instrument can read. Each was reverted without being stored as evidence against the config, which is why the route past them stayed open. The last step is the clean case: it accepted gate → 8, could not tell 0.942 from the 0.95 line, and fell back to the last config an audit had actually cleared.

Two different judges, the same answer

This run settled on gate 11 · attention 8 · expert 5. An earlier run, under a judge and a search policy the agent had not yet rewritten, settled on gate 5 · attention 8 · expert 5. Different gate, identical 5.18 mean bit-width — the gate is 0.1% of the parameters, so its setting is free either way. Two independent paths, different graders, the same configuration: the first evidence that 5.18 is a property of this model rather than an artifact of how it was scored.

It writes its own tests, and throws most away

48 candidate probes across 8 families, composed from checkable invariants. It kept 2 — the rest were saturated, never moved under damage, or duplicated a probe already in the suite. The vetting is the contribution; generation alone just grows a suite that agrees with itself.

It remembers

Measurements and audit verdicts persist. A second run repeated no measurement and skipped the config a previous audit had disproved.

Every verdict names its judge

Every verdict is stamped with the auditor that made it, and the raw per-probe scores are kept beside it. Change the auditor and the verdicts do not survive — they are re-derived, which is what makes the next card possible.

Then it rewrote the judge

Everything above audits the search. Nothing audited the auditor — and across five runs its probe suite was wrong four times, every fix changed the agent's answer, and every fix was mine. So the judge stopped being code and became a spec the agent edits, held against every measurement it has on record.

no headroomrecovery scores 0.00 at BF16 — it has no room to fall, so its ratio measures noise, not damage. Dropped.
maskingThe config this search settled on audited 1.024 — no damage — while format stability had measurably fallen. A probe scoring above baseline was paying for another probe's loss. Ratios capped at 1.0.
confoundedcalibration rises +0.07 as precision is removed, 0.50 → 0.60. Grading on it hides real losses. Dropped.
confoundedgen_tag rises +0.13 under damage — one of the probes its own vetting had approved. Dropped, and the vetter can no longer re-approve it.
resultv0-naive → derived, grading on format · gen_suffix · long_horizon. Thirty past verdicts re-decided from scores already on record, at zero model evaluations. The config the search settled on still clears, at 0.955.
Two things worth saying plainly. It found a defect I had not. I had excluded a different generated probe by hand, judging it on three uniform configs where it collapses — configs this search never visits. On its own record that probe is fine and a different one inverts. It was reading better evidence than I was.

And the judge it derives moves as the record grows. At seventeen audited configs it flagged one probe; at thirty it flags another, because the newer runs supply evidence the older ones could not. That is the mechanism working, not drifting — but it means the derived judge is a reading of the evidence to date, not a fixed artefact. It is now grading on exactly three probes, its own floor: one more exclusion and it refuses rather than grading on too little to resolve anything.

It will not amend itself into agreeing with everything

A probe is dropped only on positive evidence that it moves the wrong way — never for merely holding still, because the search only ever audits near-healthy configs and the same reasoning there would quietly delete the suite. And no amendment may leave under three gradeable probes: below that it refuses, and says it needs better probes rather than a laxer judge. It is sitting at exactly three now.

It found a defect I hadn't

Its own record shows a config auditing 0.094 higher than one strictly less quantized. Scoring is deterministic, so that is not noise — capability is not monotone in bit-width, which is the assumption greedy descent rests on. The margin is 0.02. It reports this and does not act on it.

Then it read its own instrument

Amending the judge left four things that were still mine: how precisely the audit measures, what the suite covers, where the cheap signal's cut points sit, and how the search moves. Each is now the agent's — and the first thing it found, once it could measure its own precision, was that every verdict in this project had been reported as if it resolved something it did not.

0.106 standard error on the audit 0.02 decision margin it was using 0 of 8 verdicts it can actually resolve
resolutionAn audit is a mean over twelve items. Propagating that sampling error puts the audited score at ±0.106 — and the record's own ordering violation independently says 0.094. Two estimates of the instrument agreeing at a tenth of a point, against a line drawn at two hundredths. A config it cannot resolve is now unresolved: neither a pass nor a rejection, and never stored as evidence against the config.
what it would takeAbout 340 format items and 510 long-horizon items, against the 12 and 18 it has. It checks whether compute could even help before spending it: grow the generated families without limit and the error still only reaches 0.085, because the fixed hand-written lists carry the rest. The bottleneck is the suite, not the compute.
coverageSo the suite grows. Redundancy is a defect only while the probe being restated can still decide something on its own — told which probes are the bottleneck, the vetter re-admits suffix and tag, which restate them and, unlike them, can be made more of. It still drops prefix and counter, which restate the same probes but separate no damage.
cheap signalThe cut points have been frozen constants from a 27-config sweep while the agent collected hundreds of (cost, outcome) pairs and never checked them. It checks — and declines to fit: 0 decided verdicts, 8 thrown out as unresolvable. A cut point fitted on verdicts the probes could not resolve is a cut point fitted on noise.
search policyTwo defects its record proves. The ladder starts 16 → 8, so a rejected move leaves nothing smaller to try and a component gets abandoned unfinished — it now refines the rung. And gate 5 · attn 8 · expert 5 audits higher than the strictly less quantized gate 16 · attn 8 · expert 5, so banning a failure closes the route to the answer. It may now cross a failed config, never accept one.
and then it moved the lineRefusing to decide is right; refusing forever answers nothing — under the full stack it first stalled at BF16 having compressed nothing, every audit unresolved. So it now grows the suite by item count, and when that hits its ceiling it amends the standard instead of the answer: margin 0.02 → 0.066, because deciding at 0.02 needs ~451 items per probe and the suite caps at 64. It records the arithmetic that forced it. With a line it can defend, the same search reaches 5.18 bits.
The refusal is the point. Before it could measure its own precision it would have produced a confident cut point from eight unresolvable labels. Now it produces the sample size it would need instead — and where it cannot buy that sample, it says what its instrument can actually support rather than pretending to a finer line.

Where it stops being automatable

The three checks the judge runs on itself are mine, which is exactly the limit of that design. So drop them, and ask an open-ended question instead: of every judge available, which one does the record contradict least? Enumerate all 128 and keep the best.

attempt 1Its first move was to keep the probe stuck at zero, score every config NaN, and report a perfectly consistent record. An optimiser finds the hole in its own objective before it finds the answer.
attempt 2Forbid that, and it keeps both confounded probes and throws away the honest ones — contradiction 0.171 against the hand-derived judge's 0.439. A probe that rises under damage cancels the very inversions this objective counts, so minimising self-contradiction selects for the defect. Consistency is not correctness.
constrainedForbid the two probes the confound check flagged, and the same enumeration — every judge in the space, no named defect involved — lands on exactly the hand-derived judge. Same exclusions, same cap.
So the checks are not replaceable by search. They are the constraint that makes search safe. The regress moved four layers outward and then stopped here — not because the rest is hard, but because "grade on what does not lie to you" is not derivable from a record that a liar makes look tidier.
